# Where We're Going: The Three Stages of PolySaaS

We built PolySaaS to solve a problem every growing operation eventually hits: a stack of SaaS tools that don't talk to each other, held together by brittle point-to-point integrations and the one person on the team who remembers how they all fit. That's where we started. It's not where we stop.

Here's the arc — where PolySaaS is today, and where it's headed.

## Stage One: Orchestrate What You Already Have

Right now, PolySaaS is what you've likely seen us demo: a way to take the SaaS tools you're already running — two, three, a dozen — and connect them through a real orchestration layer instead of a pile of one-off scripts. Bundled apps if you want them (Odoo, Nextcloud, Mattermost, WordPress, Dolibarr, Liferay), your existing tools if you don't. One event, routed everywhere it needs to go, without a developer on standby every time a vendor changes their API.

This stage exists to prove something simple: orchestration doesn't have to be enterprise-priced or enterprise-complicated. If you've been putting off connecting your tools because every option looked like either a $500/month iPaaS contract or a fragile Zapier chain that breaks the moment your workflow gets interesting, this is built for you.

## Stage Two: The Engine Behind Someone Else's Product

The next stage isn't visible to most people using PolySaaS, and that's the point. SaaS vendors face the same integration problem their customers do, just one level up — their subscribers want customization and integrations, and building that infrastructure in-house is slow, expensive, and not what the vendor is actually good at.

So PolySaaS runs *underneath* — embedded as the integration and customization engine inside another company's product, invisibly. The vendor's subscribers get self-service customization. The vendor gets to stay focused on their core product instead of maintaining an integration platform on the side. Nobody has to know PolySaaS is there for it to be doing the work.

This is where the parts of PolySaaS built for discovery and documentation — mapping a system's protocols and integration surface without demanding upfront API access, documenting every service as something testable and composable — really start to matter. It's infrastructure a vendor's engineering team can evaluate and trust, not just a workflow tool their ops team likes.

## Stage Three: From Connecting Apps to Building Them

The furthest stage is also the biggest shift: PolySaaS stops being only the layer that connects applications and becomes the platform you build new ones on — or the path that takes an application you already have, still running single-tenant on a server somewhere, and turns it into real multi-tenant SaaS without a rewrite.

This is where Dynamic Orchestration and Atomic Services — the same engine already handling event routing between your existing apps — extend from moving data to powering application logic itself. And it's where two of our bundled apps turn out to be a more powerful pairing than they first appear: Liferay brings enterprise-grade, multi-tenant portal architecture; WordPress brings fast, flexible content and front-end publishing. Together, sitting on top of the same orchestration layer, they give anyone building on PolySaaS a real front-end and portal foundation on day one — not a blank canvas, a starting point.

We've already started laying out this thinking in more depth — see [The Composable Enterprise](https://polysaas.online/blog) for the fuller architectural case.
